Companion · Day 1 FAQ sheet
The practical questions live next door.
Four questions came up enough to get their own sheet, faq.html. Which model to start with, and why the shelf is not all DeBERTa. How to write these scripts with an AI coding agent, and where Colab fits. Whether encoders can take long paragraphs. And whether sensitive data belongs on Colab. The reading list below is the shelf those answers point back to. Start with ref 1.

Start here · the method and the tool

1Laurer, M., van Atteveldt, W., Casas, A., & Welbers, K. (2024). Less annotating, more classifying. Addressing the data scarcity issue of supervised machine learning with deep transfer learning and BERT-NLI. Political Analysis, 32(1), 84–100. doi.org/10.1017/pan.2023.20Why. The exact zero-shot BERT-NLI approach the workshop pipeline runs. Read it first.
2Burnham, M., Kahn, K., Wang, R. Y., & Peng, R. X. (2025). Political DEBATE. Efficient zero-shot and few-shot classifiers for political text. Political Analysis, 1–15 (online first). doi.org/10.1017/pan.2025.10028Why. Open DeBERTa classifiers tuned for political text, with a package you can drop straight into a script.
3Burnham, M. (2025). Stance detection. A practical guide to classifying political beliefs in text. Political Science Research and Methods, 13(3), 611–628. doi.org/10.1017/psrm.2024.35Why. A hands-on walkthrough of stance detection with transformer encoders, end to end.
4Wolf, T., Debut, L., Sanh, V., Chaumond, J., Delangue, C., Moi, A., et al. (2020). Transformers. State-of-the-art natural language processing. Proceedings of the 2020 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing: System Demonstrations, 38–45. doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.emnlp-demos.6Why. The paper behind the Hugging Face transformers library and the pipeline call you will write.

Choosing a model and validating it

5Timoneda, J. C., & Vallejo Vera, S. (2025). BERT, RoBERTa, or DeBERTa? Comparing performance across transformer models in political science text. The Journal of Politics, 87(1), 347–364. doi.org/10.1086/730737Why. The direct answer to the FAQ above, with benchmarks on political text.
6Bestvater, S. E., & Monroe, B. L. (2023). Sentiment is not stance. Target-aware opinion classification for political text analysis. Political Analysis, 31(2), 235–256. doi.org/10.1017/pan.2022.10Why. Why an off-the-shelf score can mislead, and how to validate against a hand-coded gold set.

Foundations and tutorials

7Wankmüller, S. (2024). Introduction to neural transfer learning with transformers for social science text analysis. Sociological Methods & Research, 53(4), 1676–1752. doi.org/10.1177/00491241221134527Why. A patient, step-by-step tutorial on transfer learning with encoders for people new to the field.
8Devlin, J., Chang, M.-W., Lee, K., & Toutanova, K. (2019). BERT. Pre-training of deep bidirectional transformers for language understanding. Proceedings of NAACL-HLT 2019, 4171–4186. doi.org/10.18653/v1/n19-1423Why. The original encoder. Skim it once to understand what every model on this list descends from.
9Grimmer, J., & Stewart, B. M. (2013). Text as data. The promise and pitfalls of automatic content analysis methods for political texts. Political Analysis, 21(3), 267–297. doi.org/10.1093/pan/mps028Why. The framing paper for the whole enterprise. Validity first, methods second.
10Grimmer, J., Roberts, M. E., & Stewart, B. M. (2022). Text as data. A new framework for machine learning and the social sciences. Princeton University Press, 360 pp.Why. The book-length reference. Keep it on the desk while you build and validate.

The generative alternative

11Ornstein, J. T., Blasingame, E. N., & Truscott, J. S. (2025). How to train your stochastic parrot. Large language models for political texts. Political Science Research and Methods, 13(2), 264–281. doi.org/10.1017/psrm.2024.64Why. The case for reaching past an encoder to a generative model, and the costs that come with it. Bridge to Day 3.
